\chapter{Advanced optimization techniques}
\label{ch:tecniche_avanzate_en}

In addition to the classic metaheuristics (LNS / SA / TS / ILS),
$\pi$Tantum integrates four classes of advanced techniques used
when the standard pipeline is not enough or when targeted
diagnostic information is needed.

\section{Hall pre-check}

\texttt{engine/diagnostics/hall\_check.py}. Hall's marriage theorem
applied to the bipartite (\textit{class}, \textit{subject}) $\to$
qualified-teachers graph as a fast pre-check (1--100\,ms) before
launching Phase A. Three violation kinds are reported:

\begin{enumerate}
\item subject supply vs demand,
\item subject coverage vs demand,
\item Hall sampling random-subset with mutually exclusive subsets.
\end{enumerate}
Exposed at three UI points: inline button on PhaseACard, row in
AdvancedTechniquesCard, section of the Statistics tab.

\section{Column Generation and advanced Branch-and-Price}

\texttt{engine/column\_generation.py}. Master LP via
\texttt{scipy.linprog} HiGHS; eight dedicated CP-SAT pricers at
distinct granularities (\texttt{teacher}, \texttt{teacher-day},
\texttt{teacher-class}, \texttt{teacher-class-subject},
\texttt{teacher-subject}, \texttt{class}, \texttt{class-day},
\texttt{day}, \texttt{curriculum}); a \texttt{branch-and-price}
mode equipped with all four scalability techniques required for
MEGA-size instances:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ryan-Foster recursive tree} with Achterberg pair
  scoring on fractional class-slot pairs, best-first node
  exploration with LP-bound pruning, depth cap 20 / 1000 nodes.
\item \textbf{Box-step dual stabilization}
  $\pi_{\text{blend}} = (1-\alpha)\pi_{\text{stable}}
  + \alpha\pi_{\text{raw}}$ with $\alpha = 0.2$, applied to both
  master variants.
\item \textbf{Column management}: EWMA of the per-column reduced
  cost over 20 iterations; when the pool exceeds 10\,000
  columns, the worst (most positive \texttt{rc\_avg}) entries
  are purged, while protecting columns currently in the LP basis
  (\(x > 0\)) and the seed columns from the iterative-diversified
  primal heuristic.
\item \textbf{Parallel pricing} via \texttt{ProcessPoolExecutor}
  (default $\lfloor \texttt{os.cpu\_count()}/2 \rfloor$ workers).
  CP-SAT is CPU-bound and the GIL is only partially released by
  the OR-tools binding, so \texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor} would not
  parallelise the bottleneck; processes do.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The CP-SAT-as-fundamental contract}

For every granularity, the sub-problem CP-SAT is the
\emph{fundamental} minimisation; greedy serves only as a
\texttt{model.add\_hint} warm-start (an accelerator). If CP-SAT
cannot find a feasible solution within the time limit the pricer
returns \texttt{(None, 0.0)}: the greedy is \emph{never} promoted
to a column. The unit test
\texttt{test\_bp\_pricers\_use\_addhint\_warmstart} monkey-patches
\texttt{CpModel.add\_hint} with a counting wrapper to enforce this
contract.

\subsection*{Measured numbers (synthetic HUGE / MEGA benchmark)}

See \texttt{tests/benchmarks/test\_bp\_huge\_mega.py}. On a
synthetic scenario calibrated to match the engine's MEGA profile
(100 classes, $\sim$174 teachers, 4 subjects, $\sim$400 cattedre)
the pipeline reaches HARD-feasibility in \textbf{5.95\,s} with
\texttt{granularity=curriculum} and all scalability techniques
enabled. On the smaller HUGE scenario (50 classes,
$\sim$95 teachers, 200 cattedre) the time is \textbf{2.57\,s}.
The synthetic scenario is structurally easy (cattedre as 4-hour
single-day blocks); on real-world instances with arbitrary day
distributions the calibration target remains 30 minutes for MEGA,
with a 60-minute hard cap.

\section{Lagrangian relaxation}

\texttt{engine/lagrangian.py}. Relaxes the inter-cluster bridge
constraints and dualises them with multipliers $\lambda_b$;
update via subgradient ascent
$\lambda_{k+1} = \max(0, \lambda_k + \alpha_k g_k)$ with
$\alpha_k = \alpha_0 / (1+k)$. Per-iteration refinement via
Simulated Annealing.
