\chapter{Advanced optimization techniques}
\label{ch:tecniche_avanzate_en}

In addition to the classic metaheuristics (LNS / SA / TS / ILS),
$\pi$Tantum integrates four classes of advanced techniques used
when the standard pipeline is not enough or when targeted
diagnostic information is needed.

\section{Hall pre-check}

\texttt{engine/diagnostics/hall\_check.py}. Hall's marriage theorem
applied to the bipartite (\textit{class}, \textit{subject}) $\to$
qualified-teachers graph as a fast pre-check (1--100\,ms) before
launching Phase A. Three violation kinds are reported:

\begin{enumerate}
\item subject supply vs demand,
\item subject coverage vs demand,
\item Hall sampling random-subset with mutually exclusive subsets.
\end{enumerate}
Exposed at three UI points: inline button on PhaseACard, row in
AdvancedTechniquesCard, section of the Statistics tab.

\section{Column Generation and advanced Branch-and-Price}

\texttt{engine/column\_generation.py}. Master LP via
\texttt{scipy.linprog} HiGHS; nine dedicated CP-SAT pricers at
distinct granularities (\texttt{teacher}, \texttt{teacher-day},
\texttt{teacher-class}, \texttt{teacher-class-subject},
\texttt{teacher-subject}, \texttt{class}, \texttt{class-day},
\texttt{day}, \texttt{curriculum}); a \texttt{branch-and-price}
mode equipped with all four scalability techniques required for
MEGA-size instances:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ryan-Foster recursive tree} with Achterberg pair
  scoring on fractional class-slot pairs, best-first node
  exploration with LP-bound pruning, depth cap 20 / 1000 nodes.
\item \textbf{Box-step dual stabilization}
  $\pi_{\text{blend}} = (1-\alpha)\pi_{\text{stable}}
  + \alpha\pi_{\text{raw}}$ with $\alpha = 0.2$, applied to both
  master variants.
\item \textbf{Column management}: EWMA of the per-column reduced
  cost over 20 iterations; when the pool exceeds 10\,000
  columns, the worst (most positive \texttt{rc\_avg}) entries
  are purged, while protecting columns currently in the LP basis
  (\(x > 0\)) and the seed columns from the iterative-diversified
  primal heuristic.
\item \textbf{Parallel pricing} via \texttt{ProcessPoolExecutor}
  (default $\lfloor \texttt{os.cpu\_count()}/2 \rfloor$ workers).
  CP-SAT is CPU-bound and the GIL is only partially released by
  the OR-tools binding, so \texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor} would not
  parallelise the bottleneck; processes do.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The CP-SAT-as-fundamental contract}

For every granularity, the sub-problem CP-SAT is the
\emph{fundamental} minimisation; greedy serves only as a
\texttt{model.add\_hint} warm-start (an accelerator). If CP-SAT
cannot find a feasible solution within the time limit the pricer
returns \texttt{(None, 0.0)}: the greedy is \emph{never} promoted
to a column. The unit test
\texttt{test\_bp\_pricers\_use\_addhint\_warmstart} monkey-patches
\texttt{CpModel.add\_hint} with a counting wrapper to enforce this
contract.

\subsection*{Measured numbers (synthetic HUGE / MEGA benchmark)}

See \texttt{tests/benchmarks/test\_bp\_huge\_mega.py}. On a
synthetic scenario calibrated to match the engine's MEGA profile
(100 classes, $\sim$174 teachers, 4 subjects, $\sim$400 cattedre)
the pipeline reaches HARD-feasibility in \textbf{5.95\,s} with
\texttt{granularity=curriculum} and all scalability techniques
enabled. On the smaller HUGE scenario (50 classes,
$\sim$95 teachers, 200 cattedre) the time is \textbf{2.57\,s}.
The synthetic scenario is structurally easy (cattedre as 4-hour
single-day blocks); on real-world instances with arbitrary day
distributions the calibration target remains 30 minutes for MEGA,
with a 60-minute hard cap.

\section{Branch-and-Price MVP scaffold (Step 4)}
\label{sec:bp-mvp-scaffold-en}

Step 4 of the multi-day plan introduces an OO-textbook
\emph{scaffold} of the Branch-and-Price loop that tightens
three orthogonal pieces inside one module
(\texttt{engine/column\_generation.py} with
\texttt{mode="branch-and-price"}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\texttt{PhaseBDaySolver}} -- OO wrapper around the
  legacy \texttt{solve\_phase\_b\_for\_day} function. Exposes the
  object-oriented interface (\texttt{solve()}, scope access,
  \texttt{via\_dsl} augmentation) but delegates model
  construction to the proven legacy code path. Benefit: 100+
  Phase B regression tests keep passing \emph{by construction};
  with \texttt{via\_dsl=True} the legacy model is augmented
  with extra DSL constraints (DB-stored rules + extra
  expressions passed to the constructor).
\item \textbf{Ryan-Foster pricing-in-nodes} -- at every RF tree
  node the CP-SAT pricer is re-invoked with the branch
  decisions (\texttt{together} / \texttt{apart}) already
  applied to its model. This is the textbook BP, in contrast
  to the previous arrangement where nodes only re-solved the
  master LP on the existing pool.
\item \textbf{Smoke benchmark} --
  \texttt{tests/benchmarks/test\_bp\_step4\_smoke.py} runs the
  full loop (Mono $\to$ initial pool $\to$ master DW $\to$ RF
  tree $\to$ integer recovery $\to$ completion) on a
  \texttt{small} scenario (10 classes) and a \texttt{medium}
  one (28 classes), checking HARD-feasibility and that the soft
  cost does not regress against the iterative-diversified
  baseline.
\end{enumerate}

The flag \texttt{mode="branch-and-price"} activates the full
path. The previous \texttt{iterative-diversified} mode remains
the default and continues to act as a primal-heuristic seeder
of the column pool.

\section{Lagrangian relaxation}

\texttt{engine/lagrangian.py}. Relaxes the inter-cluster bridge
constraints and dualises them with multipliers $\lambda_b$;
update via subgradient ascent
$\lambda_{k+1} = \max(0, \lambda_k + \alpha_k g_k)$ with
$\alpha_k = \alpha_0 / (1+k)$. Per-iteration refinement via
Simulated Annealing.

\section{Phase A: the formal \emph{day count} model}
\label{sec:phase-a-formal-en}
\index{Phase A}\index{day count}

Phase A solves a \emph{distribution} problem: given the weekly
demand of every cattedra (typically 2--6 hours), over how many
days and with what daily load does each teacher work? The
formulation is a small CP-SAT on integer variables
\texttt{dc[t,c,s,d]} (\emph{day count}: hours of $(t,c,s)$ on
day $d$).

\begin{defbox}[title={The \texttt{DayCountModel}}]
Let $T$ be teachers, $C$ classes, $S$ subjects,
$D = \{1,\dots,6\}$ days. For each cattedra $(t,c,s) \in
\mathit{Catt}$ with weekly demand $h_{t,c,s} \in \mathbb{N}_+$
and each $d \in D$:
\[
  dc_{t,c,s,d} \in \{0, 1, \dots, h_{\max}\},\quad h_{\max}=6,
\]
subject to
\begin{align}
\text{(coverage)}\quad &\sum_d dc_{t,c,s,d} = h_{t,c,s} & \forall (t,c,s) \\
\text{(class daily load)}\quad &\sum_{(t,s)} dc_{t,c,s,d} \in \{0\}\cup [4,6] & \forall c, d \\
\text{(teacher daily load)}\quad &\sum_{(c,s)} dc_{t,c,s,d} \le h^{\text{prof}}_{\max} & \forall t, d
\end{align}
The objective combines: deviation from the ideal load
(weight\,4), weekly uniformity (weight\,3), fragmented free-day
penalty (weight\,1).
\end{defbox}

The architectural turn of Step 4: the same \texttt{DayCountModel}
that produces day counts in Phase~A can be \emph{reconstructed}
from the weekly solver (\texttt{cp\_sat\_scope=week}); the two
sources of \texttt{dc\_value} become comparable, and the cost
of Phase~A's rigidity becomes measurable.

\label{sec:phase-b-decomp-en}

\begin{proposition}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be the teacher co-occupation graph (edge if
two teachers share at least one class). Spectral decomposition
yields $k$ clusters $C_1, \dots, C_k$ with
$|E(C_i, C_j)| \le \epsilon |E|$, $\epsilon$ proportional to
the second eigenvalue $\lambda_2(L_{\mathrm{sym}})$.
Consequence: fewer bridges for the stitch step, less latent
infeasibility across clusters.
\end{proposition}

\section{Branch-and-Price: anatomy of one iteration}
\label{sec:bp-anatomy-en}
\index{Branch-and-Price}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{manual/figures/fig_bp_components}
\caption{Five composable scalability techniques around the
\emph{Branch-and-Price} loop: Ryan-Foster recursive tree,
box-step dual stabilization, column management, parallel
pricing, pricing-in-nodes (Step~4). All five are on by default
in \texttt{mode="branch-and-price"}.}
\label{fig:bp-components-en}
\end{figure}

The modern formulation of Branch-and-Price unifies
\emph{column generation} and \emph{branch-and-bound} under one
framework~\parencite{barnhart1998,vanderbeck2000}.
Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition~\parencite{dantzigwolfe1960} is
its backbone: the original problem is rewritten in terms of
\emph{patterns} satisfying local constraints (subproblem),
and the \emph{master} picks a convex combination meeting the
global ones. Desaulniers, Desrosiers and
Solomon~\parencite{desaulniers2005} remain the applied
reference.

\subsection{Master LP (Dantzig-Wolfe)}

\begin{align}
\min \quad & \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}} c_j\, \lambda_j \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_j a_{ij}\, \lambda_j \ge b_i & \forall i \in \text{cover}\\
& \lambda_j \ge 0
\end{align}

$c_j$ = pattern cost, $a_{ij}$ = hours of cattedra $i$
covered by pattern $j$, $b_i$ = weekly demand of cattedra $i$.
The dual vector $\pi$ guides the pricer.

\subsection{Ryan-Foster recursive tree}

When the master's solution is fractional, two patterns share
the same pair $(i, j)$ at different rates. Ryan and
Foster~\parencite{ryanfoster1981} pick the active pair with
maximum Achterberg score~\parencite{achterberg2005} (fraction
$\times$ RHS contribution) and create two children:
\textbf{together} ($\lambda$-patterns must include both hours)
and \textbf{apart} (must exclude one).

\begin{schemabox}[title={Pseudocode -- Ryan-Foster recursive tree}]
\sffamily\small
\textbf{procedure} \texttt{rf\_branch}($\text{pool}$, $\text{depth}$):\\
\hphantom{XX} if $\text{depth} > $ MAX\_DEPTH ($=20$): \textbf{return} best\_int\\
\hphantom{XX} solve master LP on $\text{pool}$ $\to$ $\lambda^*, \pi^*$\\
\hphantom{XX} if $\lambda^*$ integral: \textbf{return} $\lambda^*$\\
\hphantom{XX} pick $(i_*, j_*)$ with max Achterberg score\\
\hphantom{XX} $\text{pool}_T \gets \texttt{branch\_together}(\text{pool}, i_*, j_*)$\\
\hphantom{XX} $\text{pool}_A \gets \texttt{branch\_apart}(\text{pool}, i_*, j_*)$\\
\hphantom{XX} return best of \texttt{rf\_branch}($\text{pool}_T$, $\text{depth}+1$),\\
\hphantom{XXXX} \texttt{rf\_branch}($\text{pool}_A$, $\text{depth}+1$)
\end{schemabox}

\begin{theorem}[Branch-and-Price termination]
If CP-SAT pricers always terminate (column found or proved
absent) and Ryan-Foster has finite depth cap, the BP loop
terminates in finite time with an integer solution optimal for
the cover relaxation.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Box-step dual stabilization}

Master duals oscillate between iterations (degeneracy). The
box-step scheme keeps a smoothed
$\pi_{\text{stable}}$ and feeds the pricers
$\pi_{\text{blend}} = (1-\alpha)\pi_{\text{stable}} +
\alpha \pi_{\text{raw}}$ with $\alpha = 0.2$.

\subsection{EWMA column management}

After $50$ iterations a \texttt{teacher} pricer hits
$\sim 3000$ columns; $80\%$ are inactive. \texttt{piTantum}
keeps a $20$-iter EWMA of each column's reduced cost and, when
the pool exceeds $10\,000$, purges the worst while preserving
(a) columns active in the master basis and (b) seed columns.
Steady-state pool size: $\sim 8\,000$ columns.

\subsection{Parallel pricing}

CP-SAT pricers across granularities are independent: the BP
loop runs them in parallel via \texttt{ProcessPoolExecutor}
with \texttt{workers = $\lfloor$cpu\_count() / 2$\rfloor$}.

\subsection{Pricing-in-nodes (Step 4)}

Step 4 introduces \emph{pricing-in-nodes}: at each RF tree
node the pricer is re-launched with branch decisions
(\texttt{together}/\texttt{apart}) already applied to its
CP-SAT model. The benefit: search space at each depth shrinks
\emph{structurally}, and the quality of generated columns
grows.

\section{Metaheuristics: a decision matrix}
\label{sec:meta-decision-matrix-en}
\index{metaheuristics}\index{ALNS}

\texttt{piTantum}'s metaheuristics are post-processing: take a
HARD-feasible solution and polish the SOFT. All respect
\texttt{is\_hard\_feasible} and delegate micro-fix to
\texttt{PhaseBDaySolver(\_cp\_repair)}.

The canonical treatment of ALNS is Pisinger and
Ropke~\parencite{pisinger2010,ropke2006_orig}; the surveys of
Burke and Petrovic~\parencite{burke2002} and
Pillay~\parencite{pillay2014} are the standard references for
educational timetabling at large.

\subsection{The tightness criterion}

\texttt{piTantum} computes a \emph{tightness score} that
measures how tight the available slots are versus demand:
\[
  \text{tightness} = \frac{\sum_t h_t}{|T| \cdot 6 \cdot 6 \cdot \rho},
\]
where $\rho \in [0,1]$ is the average per-teacher slot
availability after applying unavailabilities. Empirical guide:
\begin{itemize}
\item tightness $< 0.4$: Phase~B alone, no metaheuristics.
\item tightness $\in [0.4, 0.6]$: $+$\,SA, $\sim 10\%$ SOFT win.
\item tightness $> 0.6$: $+$\,ALNS necessary; cascade SA $\to$
  ALNS $\to$ TS, $5$\,min budget.
\item tightness $> 0.8$: likely HARD-infeasibility; pre-flight
  Hall check mandatory.
\end{itemize}
