\label{ch:vincoli_en}

HARD: no overlap (teacher in one class at a time, class with one teacher per slot), hour coverage of all cattedre, daily start at h=8, consecutive hours, presence at h=11 (the H1/H2/H3 trio). \section{Plessi: multiple sites in the same school}
\label{sec:plessi_en}

In Italy many secondary schools are spread across multiple
physical sites -- the \emph{Sede Centrale} (main building),
one or more \emph{succursali} (branch buildings, e.g.
\emph{Succursale Via Garibaldi}), or a dedicated
\emph{plesso} for a specific track (e.g. \emph{Plesso ITT}
for the technical track). Moving teachers or classes
between sites during the day costs real time and introduces
constraints that cannot be modelled with H1/H2/H3 alone.

The \textbf{Plessi} module (tab \texttt{/plessi}) handles:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{site registry} (name, code, address);
\item \textbf{classroom $\to$ plesso} association (each
classroom belongs to exactly one site);
\item two kinds of behavioural rules:
\textbf{commuting rules} (minimum gap to move between two
sites) and \textbf{entity policies} (global constraints on
how many sites a teacher or class may attend).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Commuting rules: the gap between two sites}

A commuting rule applies to an ordered pair
\texttt{(from\_plesso, to\_plesso)} and specifies the
minimum number of hours that must separate two consecutive
lessons of the same entity when the classroom changes site.
Relevant fields:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{from\_plesso\_id}, \texttt{to\_plesso\_id}:
the site pair.
\item \texttt{entity\_kind}: who is affected
(\texttt{teacher}, \texttt{class}, \texttt{group}).
\item \texttt{entity\_id}: optional -- if NULL the rule is
\emph{kind-wide} (applies to every teacher / class / group);
if set it applies only to that specific entity.
\item \texttt{min\_gap\_hours}: minimum number of empty
hours required between the last lesson at site A and the
first lesson at site B.
\item \texttt{symmetric}: if \texttt{true} the rule applies
in the opposite direction (B$\to$A) as well.
\item \texttt{priority}: tie-breaker when several rules
overlap.
\end{itemize}

Typical Italian example: between \emph{Sede Centrale} and
\emph{Succursale Via Garibaldi} a 60-minute travel time is
required. The matching rule is
\texttt{(Centrale, Garibaldi, kind=teacher,
entity\_id=NULL, min\_gap\_hours=1, symmetric=true)}: no
teacher may have a lesson at Garibaldi at h=9 if they have
one at Centrale at h=8.

Rule resolution always applies \emph{specific beats
generic}: if a rule with \texttt{entity\_id=42} exists for
teacher Rossi, it overrides the kind-wide rule. Among rules
of equal specificity, higher \texttt{priority} wins.

\subsection{Entity policies: per-person global constraints}

An entity policy is a \emph{global} constraint (not tied to
a specific site pair) on the behaviour of a teacher or
class. Three policies are supported:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{any} -- no global constraint, only commuting
rules apply. This is the default.
\item \texttt{single\_plesso\_per\_day} -- on any given day
the entity may have lessons in at most one site. Stricter
than commuting rules: even with a 1-hour gap, switching
sites mid-day is forbidden.
\item \texttt{single\_plesso\_total} -- the entity attends
\emph{only} site \texttt{plesso\_id} for the entire week.
Every classroom assigned to that entity must belong to
that site.
\end{itemize}

Concrete Italian examples:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Teacher Rossi does not want mid-day site
switches}: the office creates a policy
\texttt{(entity\_kind=teacher, entity\_id=Rossi,
policy=single\_plesso\_per\_day)}. On Monday all of
Rossi's hours are at Centrale; on Tuesday all at
Garibaldi; never a mixed day.
\item \emph{Class 3A (technical track) attends only Plesso
ITT}: policy \texttt{(entity\_kind=class,
entity\_id=3A, policy=single\_plesso\_total,
plesso\_id=ITT)}. Every classroom that hosts 3A lessons
must belong to Plesso ITT.
\item \emph{All teachers, by default, avoid mid-day site
switches}: policy \texttt{(entity\_kind=teacher,
entity\_id=NULL, policy=single\_plesso\_per\_day)} --
applies to every teacher except those with a more
specific override.
\end{itemize}

Entity policies follow the same \emph{specific beats
generic} rule (per-entity override), then \texttt{priority}.

\subsection{Pre-run validation}

Before launching the optimisation the system runs a battery
of checks on the plessi data
(\texttt{validate\_plessi\_rules}). If anything is
inconsistent the run is rejected with a list of violations.
What is checked:

\begin{itemize}
\item empty or duplicate plesso code;
\item commuting rule referencing a non-existent plesso;
\item commuting rule with negative \texttt{min\_gap\_hours}
or larger than the day length;
\item duplicate kind-wide commuting rule (same site pair,
same kind, NULL entity\_id, defined twice);
\item \texttt{single\_plesso\_total} entity policy with no
\texttt{plesso\_id} or with a non-existent site;
\item entity policy with an unknown \texttt{policy} value;
\item override for a non-existent entity (deleted teacher
or class).
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{GET /api/plessi/validate} endpoint lets the UI
display a live ``plessi config OK / N issues'' badge.

\subsection{CP-SAT implementation}

At solver level, commuting rules become \emph{pairwise}
constraints on the classroom-assignment variables:

\[
\forall (h_a, h_b)\ \text{adjacent such that}\
\text{room}(t, d, h_a) \in P_A,\
\text{room}(t, d, h_b) \in P_B:\
h_b - h_a \geq \text{min\_gap}_{P_A \to P_B}.
\]

Entity policies become counter constraints:
\texttt{single\_plesso\_per\_day} is
$\sum_{P} \mathbf{1}[\exists h:\,\text{room}(t,d,h) \in P]
\leq 1$ for each day $d$;
\texttt{single\_plesso\_total} fixes the domain of the
classroom variables to the target site only.

All such constraints are \emph{hard} by default. The
roadmap envisages a \emph{soft} variant with penalty for
sporadic emergency overrides (e.g. last-minute substitute
teaching), but the current version treats every plesso
constraint as HARD.

\subsection{Plessi via DSL (Step 3a-3c)}

Starting in Step 3a, \texttt{PlessoCommutingRule} rows are
fully expressible as DSL clauses thanks to two language
additions (cf.\ cap.~\ref{ch:dsl_generico_per_i_vincoli_en}):

\begin{itemize}
\item the \texttt{l.classroom.plesso} chained path that, given
  a lesson, resolves the assigned room and then the plesso the
  room belongs to;
\item the \texttt{consecutive(l1.slot, l2.slot)} predicate,
  true when the two lessons fall on the same day and differ
  by one hour.
\end{itemize}

The legacy rule ``Centrale $\to$ Garibaldi minimum 1-hour gap,
teacher Rossi'' translates to:

\begin{lstlisting}
forall l1 in lessons where
       l1.classroom.plesso == 1
   and l1.teacher == "Rossi":
    forall l2 in lessons where
           l2.classroom.plesso == 2
       and l2.teacher == "Rossi"
       and consecutive(l1.slot, l2.slot):
        false
\end{lstlisting}

The helper \texttt{plesso\_commuting\_rule\_to\_dsl(rule)} in
\texttt{engine/dsl\_translator.py} produces these clauses
automatically from the ORM row. A regression test enforces
zero-drift: the DSL path emits exactly the same forbidden slot
pairs as the legacy \texttt{plessi\_constraints.py} pipeline.

\section{Teacher free-day preferences}
\label{sec:vincoli_free_day_en}

Italian school contracts (CCNL) grant every full-time
teacher at least one weekday off. $\pi$Tantum models this
with a two-layer architecture: a \emph{universal HARD floor}
``at least $n$ free days per week'' plus a \emph{three-tier
priority-ordered SOFT preference} system.

\paragraph{HARD floor.} The number $n_t$ is per-teacher,
loaded from the column \texttt{Teacher.min\_free\_days}
(Integer, NOT NULL, DEFAULT 1). Typical values:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $n_t = 1$: full-time CCNL (default; about 70\% of
    teachers in the stress profiles).
  \item $n_t = 2$: split / reduced timetable (about 25\%).
  \item $n_t = 3$: heavy part-time (about 5\%).
  \item $n_t = 0$: constraint disabled (rare; typically
    short-term substitutes).
\end{itemize}
For every teacher the DSL translator emits
\begin{verbatim}
teacher_at_least_n_free_days("Rossi", 2)
\end{verbatim}
The compiler translates this to
$\sum_d \mathbf{1}\{t\text{ busy on day }d\} \le |D| - n_t$,
where the busy indicator is built from the slot Bools
(Phase B / monolithic) or the day\_count IntVars (Phase A
fallback). The pragma is registered with
\texttt{level=both} so it applies uniformly across the
\texttt{cpsat\_week}, \texttt{cpsat\_day\_skip\_phase\_a}
and \texttt{cpsat\_day\_soft\_hint} bypass techniques.
The legacy Phase-A-only \texttt{free\_day\_choice\_3way}
pragma was silently dropped by these bypasses, producing
\texttt{hard\_violation} rows on the v2 SQLite bench; the
new pragma fixes the asymmetry.

\paragraph{Feasibility note.} With \texttt{max\_per\_day} = 2
and $n_t = 3$ the teacher's weekly hours must fit in
$|D| - n_t = 3$ working days: 5 hours work (2+2+1), 7 do
not (theoretical max 6). The stress profiles avoid
combining $n_t = 3$ with cattedre exceeding 6 hours. If
the user picks values incompatible with the weekly load,
the solver returns \texttt{INFEASIBLE} -- there is no
silent fallback.

\paragraph{SOFT priority preferences.} On top of the
floor, every teacher has up to three preferred free days
in the dedicated \texttt{teacher\_free\_day\_preferences}
table (columns \texttt{teacher\_id}, \texttt{day} 1..6,
\texttt{priority} 1..3). The penalty is fixed by priority:
priority 1 weighs 30 (top choice -- the most expensive to
violate), priority 2 weighs 20, priority 3 weighs 10. The
formula is $w_p = 40 - 10 p$. Per row the translator emits
\begin{verbatim}
teacher_preferred_free_day_penalty("Rossi", 6, 30)
\end{verbatim}
and the compiler contributes $w_p \cdot
\mathbf{1}\{t\text{ busy on day }d\}$ to the SOFT
objective. Under minimisation the solver tends to honor
priority 1 first because ignoring it costs the most.

\paragraph{Worked examples for $N \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.}

\textit{$N = 1$ (full-time)}. A teacher with 5 weekly hours
and preferences [Mon pri.1, Tue pri.2, Wed pri.3] pays
$30\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Mon}} + 20\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Tue}} +
10\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Wed}}$. Leaving Monday free costs 0;
leaving Tuesday free costs 30; leaving Wednesday free
costs 50. The HARD floor forces $\ge 1$ free day, so
Monday is the rational pick unless other HARDs (e.g. a
fixed coteach lesson on Monday) make it infeasible.

\textit{$N = 2$ (split timetable)}. Same preferences,
4 weekly hours. The HARD floor forces \emph{two} free
days, so the teacher works at most 4 days. Under the SOFT
priorities the optimum leaves Mon \& Tue free (cost 10:
only priority 3 pays). The 4 hours spread over Wed..Sat,
one per day.

\textit{$N = 3$ (heavy part-time)}. Same preferences,
5 weekly hours. The HARD floor forces \emph{three} free
days, so the teacher works at most 3 days. With
\texttt{max\_per\_day} = 2 the 5 hours pack as $2 + 2 + 1$;
the solver picks the three days that leave Mon, Tue, Wed
free (SOFT cost 0). With 7 hours instead of 5, the
combination $N = 3$ + \texttt{max\_per\_day} = 2 would be
\texttt{INFEASIBLE} (capacity $3 \times 2 = 6 < 7$).

\paragraph{Try it (SQL).}
\begin{verbatim}
-- HARD floor per teacher:
SELECT t.name, t.min_free_days
  FROM teachers t ORDER BY t.id;

-- stratified distribution in the stress profiles
-- (expected 70/25/5 split):
SELECT min_free_days, COUNT(*)
  FROM teachers GROUP BY min_free_days;

-- 3 SOFT priority-ordered preferences:
SELECT t.name, p.day, p.priority,
       40 - 10*p.priority AS weight
  FROM teacher_free_day_preferences p
  JOIN teachers t ON t.id = p.teacher_id
 ORDER BY t.name, p.priority;
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Edge case: HARD per-day.} Part-time teachers
who must by contract be off on a specific day still use
the separate \texttt{teacher\_mandatory\_free\_days} table.
Each row becomes a HARD ``zero lessons on day X'' rule
that composes with the universal floor and the SOFT
priority preferences without conflict.

\section{Canonical patterns and seeded legacy HARDs (Step 3b-3e)}

Step 3b-3e introduces a vocabulary of canonical patterns
recognised directly by the DSL $\to$ CP-SAT compiler. Each
maps to a legacy HARD constraint that previously lived only as
hardcoded encoding. They can now be authored in DSL and are
also generated automatically by
\texttt{seed\_implicit\_hardcoded(profs)}.

Accepted pragmas:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{no\_holes\_class("3B")} -- no gaps in 3B's weekly
  schedule (legacy \texttt{add\_class\_no\_holes}).
\item \texttt{class\_present\_at\_hour("3B", 11)} -- if 3B has
  any lesson on a day, the slot at hour 11 is busy (HARD-3,
  ``no exit before noon'').
\item \texttt{class\_day\_load\_in("3B", 0, 4, 5, 6)} -- 3B's
  daily load is 0 (free day) or in [4, 6] hours (HARD-1
  + HARD-2).
\item \texttt{teacher\_max\_per\_day("Rossi", 5)} -- teacher
  Rossi has at most 5 hours on any day.
\item \texttt{cattedra\_max\_per\_day("Rossi", "3B",
  "Matematica", 2)} -- the (Rossi, 3B, Math) cattedra has at
  most 2 hours per day.
\item \texttt{subject\_pair\_must("3B", "Scienzemotorie")} --
  when 3B has 2 hours of PE on a day they must be consecutive.
\item \texttt{subject\_pair\_exists("3B", "Matematica")} --
  when 3B has 2+ hours of math on a day at least one
  consecutive pair must exist.
\end{itemize}

Practical effect: the advanced user can now write
\texttt{no\_holes\_class("4A")} in the ``+ New DSL constraint''
modal and obtain the same outcome as the
\texttt{hard\_no\_holes} toggle in \texttt{school\_classes}.
The DSL advantage is composability with \texttt{forall},
\texttt{exists}, \texttt{count}: the rule can be restricted to
subsets of classes, combined with other predicates and modified
on the fly without editing the database.
